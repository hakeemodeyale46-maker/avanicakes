# Fix Playbook

Every fix must be concrete enough that an editor could execute it without asking a question.

## What can be changed, and when

| Change | Already-published video | Next video |
|---|---|---|
| Title | ✅ Yes (and A/B test if the platform supports it, e.g. YouTube "Test & compare") | ✅ |
| Thumbnail / cover | ✅ Yes (YouTube; limited on some short-form platforms) | ✅ |
| Description / caption / hashtags | ✅ Yes | ✅ |
| Chapters | ✅ Yes (YouTube, via timestamps in description) | ✅ |
| Trim start/end, cut middle sections | ⚠ Sometimes (YouTube Studio editor can trim/cut; big edits may take time; short-form platforms generally no) | ✅ |
| Add footage, re-order, re-record, new hook | ❌ Requires re-upload (usually not worth it for a video with traction; sometimes worth it for a flop — note that re-uploads start from zero) | ✅ |
| Pinned comment, end screens, cards | ✅ Yes | ✅ |

Always label each fix: `THIS VIDEO`, `NEXT VIDEO`, or `BOTH`.

## Fix card format (required)

```
[#] <name>                            Priority: HIGH/MED/LOW   Confidence: H/M/L
Where:   <timestamp range | script line | title | thumbnail>
Problem: <viewer-level description>
Evidence:<data point and/or content observation>
Fix:     <exact action; for words, write the words>
Why:     <psychology reason>
Expect:  <metric that should move + how to verify>
Scope:   THIS VIDEO | NEXT VIDEO | BOTH
```

Priority = (expected impact × confidence) ÷ effort. Fixes to the **first failing funnel stage**
come first.

## Packaging fixes

**Title**
- Make the promise specific and visual: numbers, stakes, contrast, outcome.
- Front-load the interesting part (titles are truncated on mobile).
- One idea per title. If it needs "and", it's two videos.
- Match the viewer's language (search terms for search traffic; emotional hooks for browse).
- Avoid vague words: "amazing", "crazy", "insane" without a concrete noun.
- Write 3–5 options with different angles (curiosity, outcome, stakes, contrast, how-to).

**Thumbnail**
- Readable at ~1 inch wide. One focal point. High contrast. ≤ 3–4 words of text (or none).
- Complements the title — don't repeat the title's words; add information.
- Show the most compelling moment/outcome or the tension right before it.
- Faces with a clear emotion help in many niches; test rather than assume.
- The thumbnail's image must appear in the video, early or clearly foreshadowed.

**Short-form first frame**
- The first frame *is* the thumbnail in feeds. Start mid-action, with movement, and with
  on-screen text stating the hook.

## Hook fixes (the highest-leverage edits)

Long-form (first 15–30s):
1. **Delete the preamble.** No logo, no "welcome back", no subscribe ask, no sponsor.
2. **Confirm the promise immediately** — show/say the thing the title/thumbnail promised within
   the first ~5 seconds.
3. **Add stakes or a question** — why this matters, what could go wrong, what they'll get.
4. **Preview the payoff** — flash-forward to the best moment without fully giving it away.
5. **Start in motion** — first shot has movement or an event, not a static setup.
6. **Get to the first "delivery" fast** — the first real value by ~30–60s.

Short-form (first 1–3s):
1. First frame: action or striking visual, not a face saying "so".
2. On-screen text stating the hook in ≤ 8 words.
3. First spoken line = the hook. Cut every word before it.
4. Pose the question the ending answers.
5. Make the loop: the last line/visual flows back into the first.

Hook templates (adapt; always keep them true):
- **Outcome first**: "This cake cost $12 and it beat a $300 bakery cake. Here's how."
- **Stakes**: "If this layer slides, the whole order is ruined — and it's due in two hours."
- **Contrast**: "Beginners do this. Professionals do this."
- **Question**: "Why do homemade cakes always sink in the middle?"
- **Mistake**: "You're probably ruining your frosting in the first 30 seconds."
- **In medias res**: open on the dramatic moment, then "Let's back up."
- **Challenge**: "I have 10 minutes to make a wedding cake."

Always give 2–3 rewritten options in the creator's voice.

## Body fixes

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Steady slide | Increase density: cut 10–30% of runtime; remove repeated points; tighten pauses (jump cuts); add open loops every 30–90s |
| Staircase at section ends | End each section with a forward hook ("but that wasn't the real problem…"); remove "that's it for X" language |
| Long talking-head stretch | Add B-roll, zoom punch-ins, on-screen text, graphics, cutaways every few seconds (short-form) or ~5–15s (long-form) |
| Confusion spike | Slow down; add on-screen labels; show before telling; define terms; add a quick diagram |
| Repetition | Cut the second explanation; if a recap is needed, make it visual and fast |
| Sponsor drop | Move sponsor after a strong open loop is set; shorten; integrate a transition; never right before the payoff |
| Payoff too early | Move the reveal later, and/or add a bigger second question ("but the real test is…") |
| False ending | Remove "in conclusion/finally/that's it"; replace with a hook to the last section |
| Low energy segment | Cut it, compress it into a montage, or re-record with more energy |
| Order problem | Put the most interesting content earlier; escalate (good → better → best) |
| Chapters causing skips | Rename chapters to create curiosity rather than giving away answers, or merge them |

## Payoff & ending fixes

- Deliver the promise fully and clearly — the "did you get what you came for?" moment.
- End within seconds of the payoff. Long outros tank AVD with no benefit.
- Put the CTA (subscribe, next video, product, booking) **at or right after the peak
  moment**, in one sentence tied to the content ("If you want the recipe for the frosting
  that saved this cake, it's in the next video →").
- Long-form: link a specific next video with a reason to watch it (session continuation).
- Short-form: design a loop — last frame/line leads naturally into the first.

## Satisfaction fixes

- Give viewers a reason to comment: ask a specific, easy question tied to the video.
- Share-worthy moment: a surprising fact, a strong opinion, a relatable moment, a
  transformation.
- Save-worthy content: lists, recipes, steps, templates — tell them it's worth saving.
- Consistency with the channel promise — so returning viewers know what they'll get.

## Anti-patterns (don't recommend)

- Clickbait that the video doesn't deliver.
- Fake countdowns, fake stakes, fake "part 2" teasers with no part 2.
- Engagement bait ("comment 'yes' if…") as a primary tactic.
- Random cuts/sound effects with no purpose ("attention" without meaning fatigues viewers).
- Chasing a universal "ideal length" — the ideal length is the shortest length that fully
  delivers the promise.
- Generic advice not tied to a timestamp or content observation.

## Next-video experiment design

When recommending changes for future videos, frame them as testable experiments:
- Change **one major variable** at a time when possible (hook style, length, thumbnail style).
- Define the metric that should move (e.g., 0:30 retention, CTR at comparable impressions).
- Compare over 3–5 videos, not one.
- Keep a simple log: video, variable changed, result.
