# Pacing: Too Fast, Too Slow, Too Much, Too Little

Pacing is how the viewer *feels* time passing. Good pacing means the viewer is always
receiving something new at a rate they can take in. Bad pacing is one of the most common
reasons people swipe away, and it's often the hidden cause behind a dip that "looks fine" in
the transcript.

Pacing has **two separate dials**. Always judge both.

| Dial | Question | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed** (tempo) | How fast do things *change*? | Cut frequency, words per minute, how fast new information arrives, how long shots are held |
| **Density** (load) | How much is happening *at the same time*? | Number of simultaneous layers: voice, music, sound effects, on-screen text, captions, stickers, motion, B-roll, zooms |

A video can be slow *and* overloaded (long static shot with three text boxes, music and a
voiceover saying something different from the text), or fast *and* empty (quick cuts of
nothing in particular). They need different fixes.

## The pacing grid

|  | **Too little going on** | **Right amount** | **Too much going on** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Too slow** | **Boredom.** Static shots, dead air, slow talking, nothing new. Viewer: *"Nothing's happening."* → steady slide, early exits | **Drag.** Content is fine but it's stretched. Viewer: *"Get on with it."* → gradual slide, skip-ahead | **Muddle.** Lingering but cluttered: text walls, competing audio. Viewer: *"What am I meant to look at?"* |
| **Right speed** | **Thin.** Moves at a good speed but each beat has no substance. Viewer: *"Where's the point?"* | ✅ **Flow.** Viewer is always getting something new at a rate they can absorb | **Busy.** Good tempo but every frame is stuffed. Viewer: tiring, misses key info |
| **Too fast** | **Empty hype.** Rapid cuts, SFX, zooms, but nothing to hold on to. Viewer: *"Flashy but meaningless."* → can hold for a few seconds then collapse | **Rushed.** Solid content delivered faster than it can be understood. Viewer: *"Wait, what?"* → rewinds (spikes) or leaves (confusion) | **Overload.** Too fast AND too much. Viewer: overwhelmed; older or casual viewers bail first |

## Symptoms in the data

| Symptom | Likely pacing problem |
|---|---|
| Steady, steep slide with no clear event | Too slow / too little (boredom or drag) |
| Early hold then collapse after ~5–10s | Fast-but-empty hook — the hype promised more than the body delivers |
| Spikes (rewinds) at fast sections | Too fast / rushed — people rewinding to catch what they missed |
| Drop where text appears on screen | Text too long to read in time, or too much on screen at once |
| Drop at a shot held too long | Too slow for that platform's audience |
| Low completion but high rewatches | Dense content people value but can't absorb in one pass → slow down or cut down |
| Comments like "what did she say?", "too fast", "can't read it" | Rushed / overload |
| Comments like "get to the point", "skip to 0:40" | Drag |
| Good watch time on FB, poor on TikTok for the same video | Pacing tuned for older/slower audience — needs a faster cut for TikTok |
| Good on TikTok, poor on FB | Too fast/dense for FB audience — needs a slower, clearer cut |

## How to measure pacing (from a video or transcript)

Split the video into beats (each new idea, shot, or moment). For each beat, record:

1. **Shot length** — seconds until the next cut or significant visual change.
2. **Speech rate** — words per minute (count words ÷ seconds × 60).
3. **Information rate** — new facts/story beats per 10 seconds.
4. **Layers** — count what's active at once: voice, music, SFX, captions, extra text,
   stickers/emojis, camera motion, on-screen motion, zooms, split screens.
5. **Reading load** — characters of on-screen text and how long it's on screen.
6. **Dead time** — silence, pauses, "um", repeated words, setup with no payoff.

Then compare with the platform bands below.

### Reference bands (heuristics — adjust to niche and the creator's own data)

| | TikTok | Instagram Reels | Facebook (esp. older audience) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical shot length (edit-driven videos) | ~0.5–2.5s | ~1–3s, cuts often on the beat | ~2–5s; let key shots breathe |
| Talking-to-camera speech | Fast, energetic, ~160–190+ wpm OK | ~150–175 wpm | ~130–155 wpm, clear diction |
| Visual change in a talking shot | Every ~2–4s (punch-in, B-roll, text) | Every ~2–4s | Every ~4–7s |
| Layers at once | 2–4 (voice + captions + motion + 1 extra) | 2–3, cleaner, aesthetic | 1–3: one main visual + one clear text line (+ voice) |
| On-screen text | Short, punchy, may change fast | Short, styled, readable | Larger, high contrast, one line at a time, held long |
| Hook window | ~1s | ~1–2s | ~2–3s (but must still be clear instantly — autoplay may be muted) |

These are **starting points**, not rules. Storytime, ASMR-style process videos, and emotional
videos deliberately run slower on every platform. Always prefer the creator's own best-
performing videos as the benchmark.

### Reading-time check (for every on-screen text)
Viewers must be able to read text before it disappears, while also watching the image.
- Rough minimum: **~0.3–0.4s per word + ~1s** to notice the text, for younger/fast readers.
- For older audiences and Facebook: **~0.5s per word + ~1.5s**, and fewer words.
- Subtitle-style guideline: ~15–17 characters/second is comfortable for fluent adult readers;
  aim lower (~12–14 cps) for older or non-native audiences.
- If text changes faster than this, it's **too fast**. If a long paragraph appears at once,
  it's **too much**.
- Text must not cover faces, the product (e.g., the cake), or sit in the platform's UI zones
  (bottom caption area, right-side buttons).

## Rhythm: variation beats constant speed

Constant speed — even "good" speed — becomes monotonous. Strong videos **vary** pace:
- Fast sections for setup, montage, energy, and process.
- Slow sections (a held shot, a pause, a silence) for the reveal, the emotional moment, or the
  punchline. A pause right before a reveal *increases* tension.
- The **reveal/payoff should usually be slower** than what precedes it — give it room.
- Monotony symptoms: the same cut length throughout, one music track at one energy, identical
  text style every 2 seconds.

Describe the rhythm as a line: e.g., `fast (0–4s) → slow build (4–12s) → fast montage
(12–20s) → held reveal (20–24s) → quick CTA (24–27s)`. Then judge whether each section's
speed fits its purpose.

## Audience & age

Pacing tolerance differs with age, familiarity, and context. As general tendencies (always
check the account's actual audience in insights):
- **Younger, heavy short-form users** (typical of TikTok): comfortable with fast cuts and
  dense layers; lose patience with slow setups and long holds.
- **Mixed audiences** (typical of Instagram): like polished, rhythmic, visually clean edits;
  less tolerant of clutter.
- **Older audiences** (more common on Facebook): need more time to take in each shot and read
  text; get lost with very fast cuts, stacked text, and slang; reward clarity, a clear story,
  real people, and a steady pace. They often watch *longer* when the pace suits them.

Don't stereotype — describe these as tendencies, and let the data override them.

## Pacing fixes

| Verdict | Fixes |
|---|---|
| Too slow | Cut pauses and filler words (jump cuts); remove setup the viewer can infer; shorten holds; speed up process footage (1.5–4× timelapse); start later in each shot; move the payoff earlier |
| Too fast | Hold key shots longer; slow the voiceover or remove words; put fewer ideas in the video; add a beat of pause before the reveal; spread one text box into several timed lines |
| Too little going on | Add a visual change (punch-in, angle change, B-roll, hands in frame); add a clear text line stating stakes/progress; add purposeful sound (not random SFX); add a mini-question to keep the loop open |
| Too much going on | Remove layers: drop stickers/emojis, cut SFX, lower or remove music under speech, keep only one text element at once, stop the text and voice saying different things, simplify the frame |
| Monotone rhythm | Vary cut length; add one deliberate slow moment (the reveal); change music energy at the turn; switch shot type at section changes |
| Hook fast, body slow (hype collapse) | Match body pace to the hook, or make the hook more honest about the pace to come |
| Rushed payoff | Give the reveal 2–4 extra seconds; hold on the reaction; let the final shot breathe before the loop/CTA |

Every pacing fix card must say: timestamp, current speed/density, target for the platform,
and the exact edit (e.g., "hold 0:11 shot 2.5s → 4s; delete the sparkle sticker; keep music
at −18 dB under voice").
