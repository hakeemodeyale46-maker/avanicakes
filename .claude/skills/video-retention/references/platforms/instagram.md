# Part 2 — Instagram (Reels)

Platform features and insight names change often. Use the names in the user's screenshots
when they differ from these.

## The room
- **Who's there**: broad, commonly young-adult to middle-aged; check the account's own age
  breakdown. Mix of followers (who know you) and strangers (Reels tab, Explore).
- **Mood**: visual, aspirational, social. People browse, then **send** things to friends in
  DMs. Aesthetics and "would I send this?" matter a lot.
- **Sound**: mixed — many watch with sound, many don't. Assume the video must make sense
  muted: on-screen text and captions are essential.
- **What feels native**: clean, good-looking visuals; beat-synced edits; transformations;
  satisfying process; tips; relatable humor; personal brands.

## How it gets distributed
- Surfaces: **Reels tab**, **Explore**, **Feed** (mostly followers), **profile grid**,
  **shares via DMs and Stories**.
- Signals that generally matter: watch time, likes per reach, and especially **sends/shares**
  (Instagram has publicly highlighted shares as a key signal for reaching new people),
  saves, comments, follows. Original content tends to be favored over reposted content
  with other platforms' watermarks.
- Trial Reels (where available) let you test a Reel on non-followers first — useful for
  hook/pacing experiments.

## Insights to read (typical names)
- **Views**, **accounts reached** (followers vs non-followers), **watch time**, **average
  watch time**, **skip rate** (people who scroll away in the first ~3 seconds) and how it
  compares to your typical skip rate, **retention graph** (where available), likes,
  comments, **shares/sends**, **saves**, reposts, follows, profile activity, audience
  age/gender/location.
- Key ratios: skip rate vs your typical; average watch time ÷ length; shares and saves per
  1,000 reached; % non-follower reach (low % = not breaking out).

## Hook (first ~1–2 seconds)
- First frame must be visually striking **and** clear muted: the finished cake, a dramatic
  moment, a strong text line.
- On-screen text states the hook in clean, readable styling (≤ 8 words), placed in the
  safe zone (not the bottom caption area or right-side buttons).
- The first frame doubles as the **grid cover** unless you set one — choose a cover that
  looks good in the profile grid.
- Good hook types: transformation tease, "the secret to…", aesthetic payoff shown first,
  relatable POV, "3 mistakes…".

## Pacing on Instagram
- **Speed**: fast but smoother than TikTok. ~1–3s shots, often **cut on the music beat**.
  Talking videos: visual change every ~2–4s.
- **Density**: keep it **clean** — 2–3 layers. One text element at a time, consistent
  styling. Clutter (stickers, multiple fonts, busy frames) hurts more here than on TikTok.
- **Rhythm**: music-driven. Build to a clear visual payoff and give it a held moment.
- **Common failures**: messy frames, text walls, text that disappears too fast, no beat sync
  on a music-driven edit, great visuals with no story (thin), recycled TikTok with watermark.

## Length
- Short, looping Reels (≈7–30s) are easy to complete and rewatch; tutorials and stories can
  run longer if the watch time holds. Cut to the shortest complete version.

## Endings & loops
- Seamless loops work well (the end cuts back to the start).
- Close on the most beautiful shot (screenshot/share-worthy).
- CTA: "Send this to someone who…", "Save for your next bake", "Comment 'CAKE' and I'll DM
  you the recipe" (only if you'll actually send it), "Book via link in bio".

## Satisfaction signals to design for
- **Sends/shares** — relatable moments, "tag your friend", surprising results, gift ideas.
- **Saves** — recipes, step-by-step, pricing guides, ideas lists.
- **Follows** — consistent theme, a series, a clear "this account is about X".
- **Comments** — specific questions, "which one would you pick, 1 or 2?"

## Personas for the simulated-viewer pass
1. **The Reels-tab browser** (25–35, sound mixed) — stops for beauty or relatability.
2. **The sharer** — watching while thinking "who would like this?".
3. **The follower** — knows the account, tolerates more, likes behind-the-scenes.
4. **The muted viewer** — must get everything from visuals and text.

## Low-view Instagram diagnosis
- High skip rate vs typical → first frame/hook problem.
- Reach mostly followers → not being shown to new people: weak shares/sends, low watch time,
  or non-original content (e.g., watermark).
- Good watch time but few shares/saves → nothing to *act* on; add a share or save trigger.

## Business accounts (e.g., bakery)
- Formats: reveal Reels, "packing an order", satisfying decorating, aesthetic flat-lays,
  customer reactions, "what $X gets you".
- Pair Reels with Stories (booking link sticker) and a pinned post explaining how to order.
- Local: location tag, city in text and caption.
