# Part 3 — Facebook (Reels + feed video)

Platform features and insight names change often. Use the names in the user's screenshots
when they differ from these.

## The room
- **Who's there**: overall skews **older** than TikTok and Instagram — many viewers are
  35–65+ — though Facebook Reels also reaches younger people. **Always check the account's
  actual age breakdown** in insights; don't assume.
- **Mood**: relaxed scrolling of a feed mixing friends, family, groups, pages, and videos.
  Less "swipe-trained" than TikTok. Community-minded: people **share to family and
  friends**, comment in full sentences, and tag people.
- **Sound**: videos commonly **autoplay muted in the feed**. Assume sound off. Captions and
  on-screen text must carry the story.
- **Device**: mostly phones, but screen size, eyesight, and reading speed vary more. Bigger
  text, higher contrast, and slower changes help.
- **What feels native**: real people and real stories, heartwarming moments, family,
  celebrations, local businesses, humor, nostalgia, practical tips, "how it's made",
  transformations. Heavily trend-dependent content (slang, niche TikTok sounds, in-jokes)
  often doesn't land.

## How it gets distributed
- Surfaces: **Feed** (in-line video), **Reels**, **Video tab**, **Groups**, **shares** to
  timelines, Messenger, and groups.
- Recommendations reach non-followers; shares and meaningful comments generally help
  distribution. Original content tends to be favored over reposted clips from others.
- Local businesses benefit from sharing to relevant local groups (following group rules).

## Insights to read (typical names)
- **Views**, **reach/viewers**, **3-second views**, **1-minute views** (longer videos),
  **average watch time / average minutes viewed**, **watch time**, **audience retention
  graph**, reactions, comments, **shares**, follows, link clicks/messages, audience
  **age/gender/location**, followers vs non-followers.
- Key ratios: 3-second views ÷ reach (did the first frame stop them?); average watch time ÷
  length; shares per 1,000 views; comments per 1,000 views; % non-follower reach.

## Hook (first ~2–3 seconds, but instantly clear)
- The first frame must tell the story **muted**: a clear image + a big, simple text line.
  "She baked her mom's 80th birthday cake from the original 1965 recipe."
- Plain language. No slang, no in-jokes, no unexplained trend formats.
- Emotional or practical hooks work well: family, milestones, surprise, "how it's made",
  a helpful tip, a local story.
- Faces, people, and relationships ("for her dad", "my grandmother's recipe") increase
  connection.

## Pacing on Facebook
- **Speed**: **slower and steadier** than TikTok/Instagram. Key shots held ~2–5s. Speech
  ~130–155 wpm with clear pronunciation. Visual change every ~4–7s in talking videos.
- **Density**: **1–3 layers**. One main image, one text line at a time, voice optional.
  Avoid stacked text boxes, stickers, rapid zooms, and heavy sound effects.
- **Text**: large, high-contrast, ≤ ~8 words per line, held long enough to read comfortably
  (~0.5s per word + ~1.5s). Full captions on all speech (burned-in or uploaded).
- **Rhythm**: a clear beginning → middle → end, like a short story. Let the reveal and the
  emotional moment breathe (hold 3–5s on the reveal and on reactions).
- **Common failures**: the TikTok cut posted as-is (too fast, text disappears, slang,
  requires sound); no captions; tiny text; story unclear without audio; ending too abrupt.
- **Also watch for too slow**: older doesn't mean sluggish. Dead air, long intros, and
  rambling still lose people. Clear and steady, not slow.

## Length
- Facebook viewers often watch longer when the story is clear. Reels ≈15–60s; feed videos
  of 1–3+ minutes can work for stories, tutorials, and behind-the-scenes. Longer videos
  should show value early and keep a clear story line. Still cut anything that doesn't serve
  the story.

## Endings
- Complete, satisfying ending — resolution of the story, the reveal, the reaction.
- Don't rely on loops; Facebook viewers prefer a clear end.
- CTA in plain words: "Share this with someone who loves baking", "Message us to order for
  your family's celebration", "Tag someone who needs this cake". Put the phone number/link
  in the caption or pinned comment.

## Satisfaction signals to design for
- **Shares** — heartwarming, nostalgic, family-relevant, local pride, useful tips.
- **Comments** — personal questions ("What cake did your mom make for your birthday?"),
  memories, opinions. Reply to comments — conversation is valued.
- **Messages/orders** — for businesses, direct "Message us" CTAs.

## Personas for the simulated-viewer pass
1. **The 58-year-old feed scroller** — sound off, reading glasses maybe not on, scrolling
   between family photos; stops for a clear, warm, relevant image.
2. **The family sharer** (40–65) — thinking "my sister would love this".
3. **The local customer** — lives nearby, needs a cake for an event, wants to know price,
   quality, and how to order.
4. **The younger Reels viewer** (18–34) — for Reels-tab traffic; faster expectations.

## Low-view Facebook diagnosis
- Low 3-second views ÷ reach → first frame not clear/interesting muted.
- Good 3-second views, fast drop after → story unclear without sound, too fast, or text
  unreadable.
- Views mostly from followers, few shares → nothing share-worthy for the family/community
  audience; add emotional or practical value.
- Good retention, few messages/orders (business) → CTA missing or unclear.

## Business accounts (e.g., bakery)
- Formats: customer stories ("this cake was for their 50th anniversary"), before/after,
  "how we make it", owner introduction, community events, seasonal orders, testimonials.
- Share into local community groups where allowed.
- Always include: what it is, price or range, how to order, the location.
