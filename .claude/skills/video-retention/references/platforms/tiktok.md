# Part 1 — TikTok

Platform features and insight names change often. Use the names in the user's screenshots
when they differ from these.

## The room
- **Who's there**: skews younger overall, but broad and growing older; check the account's
  own age breakdown in Viewers insights. Heavy short-form users, highly trained to swipe.
- **Mood**: entertainment-first, casual, curious, in a fast feed. Content is *served* to them
  — they didn't choose it. The default action is to swipe.
- **Sound**: usually ON. Voice, music, and trending sounds carry a lot of the video.
- **What feels native**: real people, phone-shot, direct to camera, raw reactions, trends,
  humor, storytime, "watch me do this", process, before/after. Over-polished ads can feel
  foreign and get swiped.

## How it gets distributed
- **For You** feed dominates discovery for most accounts. Videos are tested with smaller
  groups; strong early engagement from them tends to expand reach to wider groups.
- Other sources: Following, profile, search (TikTok is heavily used as a search engine —
  captions, spoken words, and on-screen text can help it be found), sounds, hashtags.
- Signals that generally matter: watch time / completion, rewatches, shares, saves, comments,
  follows, and whether people swipe away quickly.

## Insights to read (typical names)
- Video views, total play time, **average watch time**, **watched full video %**,
  **retention graph** (where viewers drop), new viewers vs returning, **traffic source**
  (For You, profile, following, search, sound, other), search terms, likes, comments,
  shares, **favorites/saves**, follows from video, viewer age/gender/location.
- Key ratios: average watch time ÷ length; watched full video %; shares and saves per 1,000
  views; % from For You (low % = struggling to reach new people).

## Hook (first ~1 second decides)
- Start mid-action. First frame = movement, a striking image, or a face mid-reaction —
  never a logo, a blank intro, or "hey guys".
- First spoken line **is** the hook. Cut every word before it.
- On-screen text hook in ≤ 6–8 words, placed away from the bottom caption area and the
  right-side buttons.
- Pose a question the ending answers, or show a result and promise the "how".
- Good hook types: result first ("This $15 cake fooled a wedding planner"), problem/mistake,
  "POV:", reaction, story opener ("A customer cancelled 2 hours before her party…"),
  trend format.

## Pacing on TikTok
- **Speed**: fastest of the three. Edit-driven videos often cut every ~0.5–2.5s; talking
  videos change something visual every ~2–4s (punch-in, B-roll, text). Remove all pauses
  and filler.
- **Density**: can handle 2–4 layers (voice + captions + motion + one extra), but avoid
  clutter that fights the main image.
- **Exceptions**: storytime, ASMR/satisfying process, and emotional pieces can run slower
  if voice energy or the visual itself holds attention.
- **Common failures**: slow first 2 seconds; "explaining what you'll do" before doing it;
  constant pace with no reveal moment; hype editing on thin content (collapse after ~5s).

## Length
- No fixed ideal — the right length is the shortest version that fully delivers. Very short
  videos (≈7–20s) are easier to complete and loop; longer ones (1–3 min+) work when the story
  or tutorial genuinely holds attention and watch time grows.
- If average watch time is a small fraction of the length, cut it down before anything else.

## Endings & loops
- End quickly after the payoff. Design a loop: last frame/line flows back into the first.
- Or end on an open question that begs a comment ("Would you have refunded her?").
- CTAs: one short line at the peak ("Follow for part 2", "Comment 'recipe'"); keep them
  honest — only promise a part 2 if it exists.

## Satisfaction signals to design for
- **Shares** — relatable, funny, surprising, "tag someone who…".
- **Saves** — recipes, tips, steps, lists.
- **Comments** — a clear, easy question or a mild debate; reply with video replies.
- **Follows** — series formats ("Day 3 of…", "Part 2") give a reason to follow.

## Personas for the simulated-viewer pass
1. **The swiper** (18–30, sound on, 50 videos deep) — needs a reason to stop within 1s.
2. **The searcher** (typed "how to stack a cake") — wants the answer fast.
3. **The niche fan** (loves baking/cake content) — will watch longer if it's craft-rich.

## Low-view TikTok diagnosis
- Very low views (e.g., stuck in a small initial test) → check: first-second hold, whether
  the video is clearly about something, sound choice, and whether it's native-feeling.
- High % from Following/profile and low % from For You → not reaching new people; hook and
  topic breadth are the levers.
- Good watch time but low shares → content is fine to watch but not worth passing on;
  add a share trigger.

## Business accounts (e.g., bakery)
- Formats: order stories, "making a $X cake", disasters and saves, customer reactions,
  price reveals, behind-the-scenes, trends adapted to cakes.
- CTA: "Link in bio to book" / "DM to order" at the reveal, plus in the caption.
- Local reach: mention the city in speech, text, and caption.
